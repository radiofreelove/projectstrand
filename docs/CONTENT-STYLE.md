# Content style

The Strand Project reads like a smart friend who did the research: short, direct, warm, and exact. The structure borrows from Smart Brevity (lead with what matters, then why, then details) and from explainer journalism (answer the questions people actually ask).

## Page structure

Every guide page follows the same order, so readers always know where they are:

1. **Kicker** (state or topic) and **headline**: what this page helps you do.
2. **The big picture:** one to three sentences with the answer.
3. **Last checked** date.
4. **Why it matters:** two or three bullets.
5. **On this page:** links to sections.
6. **Steps:** numbered cards with a Done checkbox. One action per step.
7. **Cost**, **Watch out**, and **Changed in 2026** boxes where they apply.
8. **Good questions:** collapsed question-and-answer sections ("Go deeper"). Hidden in Short version mode.
9. **Get help** for that state.
10. **Sources for this page**, generated by the build.

## Callout labels

| Label | Use it for | Template |
|---|---|---|
| The big picture | The answer, first | `m.lede()` |
| Why it matters | Stakes for the reader | `m.callout('why', ...)` |
| What it costs | Fees and waivers | `m.callout('cost', ...)` |
| Watch out | Risks and traps | `m.box('watch', ...)` |
| Changed in 2026 | New laws or policies | `m.callout('changed', ...)` |
| Go deeper | Details most people can skip | `m.deeper()` or `m.qa()` |

## Plain language rules

- Aim for grade 8 or lower. Keep sentences under 25 words; the build lists any that run longer.
- Active voice, second person: "File the petition," not "The petition must be filed."
- Define every legal term where it first appears, and add it to the word list.
- One idea per sentence, one action per step.
- Numbers as numerals: "$124," "6 months," "3 business days."
- Dates as "July 1, 2026."
- Sentence-case headings. Straight quotation marks. No em or en dashes: use a comma, a colon, parentheses, or a new sentence.
- Do not use italics for emphasis. Use bold sparingly.
- Links say where they go: "Oregon court forms page," never "click here."

## Accuracy rules

- Every fact needs a source: `src("id")` next to it, or a `fact("id")` value.
- Quote only text you opened. Keep quotes short.
- Mark interpretation with `m.badge('inference')` ("Our reading").
- Mark anything you could not confirm with `m.badge('unconfirmed')` ("Not confirmed").
- Use the state's own term when describing its law: Oregon "legal change of sex," Washington "sex designation" (birth certificate) and "gender designation" (license), Utah "sex designation." Use "gender marker" in general explanations.
- Never describe a court case as settled; give its status and the date of the source.

## Respect

- Write for trans, nonbinary, and intersex readers first. Never debate anyone's identity.
- Use "sex assigned at birth" in our own words. When an official source uses other terms, quote it and attribute it.
- Avoid assumptions about bodies, medical care, or transition steps.
- Do not use examples with real names, case numbers, or identifying details.
