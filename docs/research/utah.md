# Research memo: Utah

Checked: November 3, 2026. Labels: (unverified) = could not confirm in an official source; (inference) = our reasoning beyond the sources.

## Issue

How does a person in Utah change their legal name and the sex designation on a Utah birth certificate through the courts, and what rules apply to minors, as of November 3, 2026?

## Short answer

A name change is filed in the district court of the county where the person has been a bona fide resident for the preceding year, and is granted on proof in open court. A sex designation change on a Utah birth certificate requires a court finding, by clear and convincing evidence, of six statutory conditions. Requests that include a sex change are marked private by the courts: filed papers are not public, but the case history and public hearings are. Minors face added limits: no sex designation change before 15 years and 6 months, custodial parent or guardian permission, a guardian ad litem, a closed hearing, and an order that takes effect only at 16. A 2026 bill to amend these rules (H.B. 183) did not advance past a Senate committee.

## Authority

- **Utah Code 42-1-1** (amended by 2025 chapters 291 and 299): petition in "the district court of the county where the natural person resides," showing one year of bona fide residence in the county; an "offender, as that term is defined in Section 64-13-1, may not file"; disclose whether required to register under the Sex, Kidnap, and Child Abuse Offender Registry. **42-1-2**: the court orders any notice and may grant "upon proof in open court." [Title 42 chapter 1 PDF](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title42/C42_1800010118000101.pdf)
- **Utah Code 26B-8-111** (effective May 7, 2025; amended by 2025 chapter 299): (2)(a) clear and convincing evidence that the person (i) "is not involved in any kind of lawsuit," (ii) is not an offender under 64-13-1, (iii) is not seeking the change for listed improper purposes, (iv) "has transitioned," (v) "has outwardly expressed as the sex sought in the petition in a consistent and uniform manner for at least six months," and (vi) "suffers from clinically significant distress or impairment due to the current sex designation on the birth certificate"; (2)(b) evidence of medical history, care, or treatment, and that the sex sought is "sincerely held and part of the individual's core identity"; (2)(c) registry disclosure; (3) and (4) minors; (5) may be combined with a Title 42 name change; (6) the department issues a certificate that does not indicate amended fields unless requested, and an amendment history, and retains all amendment records; (8) applies only to Utah-issued birth certificates. [26B-8-111 PDF](https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title26B/Chapter8/C26B-8-S111_2025050720250507.pdf)
- **Utah State Courts, Petition for Name or Sex Change (Gender Marker Change)**: sex change cases marked private; five steps; MyPaperwork or forms 1158XX, 1730FA, 1731FA; Utah County PDF filings go to the Provo courthouse; suggested evidence including a licensed provider's letter about clinically significant distress; hearing; registry notice form 1703FA at least 30 days before a name change hearing; Vital Records form 902 with a certified order and a fee, more if sealed, several weeks; notify SSA (required for name changes), DLD, passport, and others. [Utah Courts page](https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/case-categories/family/name-change/sex-change.html)
- **Utah State Courts, Fees** (effective May 6, 2026): $375 general civil filing fee (Utah Code 78A-2-301(1)(a)); certified copy $4 per document plus $0.50 per page. [Fees page](https://www.utcourts.gov/en/self-help/legal-help/procedures/fees.html)
- **Utah Senate notice, March 4, 2026**: the Government Operations and Political Subdivisions Committee "has returned 2nd Sub. H.B. 183, SEX DESIGNATION AMENDMENTS ... to the Rules Committee." [Notice PDF](https://pf.utleg.gov/public-web/sessions/2026GS/comreport/HB0183S02.pdf)
- **LegiScan, HB 183 (2026)** (secondary, aggregator): filed in the House file for bills not passed, March 6, 2026. [LegiScan](https://legiscan.com/UT/bill/HB0183/2026)
- **Utah Driver License Division, Name change**: requires legal documentation of the name change; accepts sealed court documents; says nothing about sex designation. [DLD page](https://dld.utah.gov/name-change/)

## Application

- Adults: confirm eligibility, gather evidence, file through MyPaperwork or PDF forms in the county of residence, pay $375 or request a waiver, attend the hearing, then file the certified order and form 902 with Vital Records.
- The six conditions are cumulative. The undefined "any kind of lawsuit" condition can exclude people with any pending case (inference); the site tells readers in that situation to talk to a lawyer.

## Contrary authority and uncertainty

1. **H.B. 183 final status.** The official bill status page renders by JavaScript and did not load. The official committee notice shows the bill returned to Rules on March 4, 2026; the "not passed" status comes from LegiScan (secondary). Supporting inference: the official code site still serves 26B-8-111 effective May 7, 2025, and a 2026 version file was not found.
2. **The $375 fee for these petitions.** Utah lists $375 as the general civil filing fee and links its fee page from the name and sex change page; we read it as the applicable fee (inference). Secondary sources say the same.
3. **Vital Records fee.** The courts page says a fee applies, plus more if sealed; the amount was not found (unverified).
4. **DLD sex designation process.** No official DLD page found (unverified).
5. **Courts page wording.** The sex change section reads "If you are not currently committed to the custody of the Utah Department of Corrections..." which appears to be a typo; the statute bars offenders. The site follows the statute.
6. **2026 H.B. 174** ("Sex Characteristic Change Treatment Amendments") was signed March 18, 2026, according to a secondary tracker. It concerns medical treatment, not records, and is out of scope (unverified; not researched in depth).
